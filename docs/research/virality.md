# What makes small games go viral: research notes

Compiled 25 Oct 2026, 01:05–01:25 BST, for the overnight game build. Each claim is tied to a source. Where a source is a secondary analysis rather than a first-hand account, that's noted.

## 1. Case studies

### Flappy Bird (2013–14)
- One tap and instant death. "The task is clear, the feedback is immediate and clear; you mess up and die in three seconds." It looks easy, so failing feels like your own fault, and that pushes you to retry. (Scientific American, https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/be-one-with-flappy-bird-the-science-of-flow-in-game-design/)
- Nguyen said: "People in the same classroom can play and compete easily because it is simple to learn, but you need skill to get a high score." He credits score comparison and score sharing. (The Verge, https://www.theverge.com/2014/2/5/5383708/flappy-bird-revenue-50-k-per-day-dong-nguyen-interview)
- Spread: a Reddit "masochistic games" list, then PewDiePie's video (5.4M views in a week). Reaction and rage clips were the media that carried it. (Know Your Meme, https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/flappy-bird; WIRED, https://www.wired.com/2014/02/flappy-bird/)

### 2048 (2014)
- A free, open-source **web** game built over a weekend. It reached 100M plays in under three weeks, and only about 1% of games were won, so it was "elegant and an impossible challenge". (CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/2014/03/26/19-year-old-makes-viral-game-hit-in-a-weekend.html)
- Cirulli's main addition to an existing clone was **animation**: the earlier version "was a bit hard to play without them". Juice turned a clone into the hit. (Hacker News, https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7373927)
- It cloned Threes via 1024. The free, no-install version beat the $1.99 original. (TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/24/clones-clones-everywhere-1024-2048-and-other-copies-of-popular-paid-game-threes-fill-the-app-stores/)

### Wordle (2021–22)
- Went from 90 players (1 Nov) to 300,000 two months later. (NYT, https://www.nytimes.com/2022/01/14/crosswords/everyone-wants-to-be-wordle.html)
- The **spoiler-free emoji grid** was invented by a player (Elizabeth S) and built in as a Share button on 16 Dec 2021. Wardle: "obviously that's had a huge impact in helping it go viral." The grid "tells a story" and makes non-players ask "what's Wordle?" (Slate, https://slate.com/culture/2022/01/wordle-game-creator-wardle-twitter-scores-strategy-stats.html; Video Game Canon, https://www.videogamecanon.com/adventurelog/the-history-of-wordle/)
- One puzzle a day for everyone creates a **shared experience**. Most sharing happens in family and friend group chats, not public feeds: "a really low effort way of checking in". (TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/12/josh-wardle-interview-wordle/)
- Wardle left out push notifications and endless play on purpose, and scarcity helped. He also removed the link from the share text because it felt spammy. (RNZ, https://www.rnz.co.nz/national/programmes/the-weekend/audio/2018826546/josh-wardle-the-power-of-wordle; Slate)

### Suika Game (2021, viral 2023)
- Sales up "50,000 times". The developer credits "cute and friendly art", "simple rules" and "an atmosphere that encourages you to try to do better next time even if you fail". (Automaton, https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20231005-22096/)
- Analysts point to the **sliding physics**: round fruit in a square box is "a chaos engine" that produces surprise, frustration and excitement, which makes entertaining content. There's **no timer**, so streamers can talk while they play. (20 Minutes, https://www.20minutes.fr/high-tech/by-the-web/4061933-20231112-secret-derriere-succes-soudain-suika-game)
- Carried by VTubers and streamers (Twitch hours up to 2.84M a month) and TikTok near-miss clips (#スイカゲーム had 70.9M views). (GamesRadar, https://www.gamesradar.com/watermelon-game-is-the-switchs-latest-viral-sensation-and-its-booming-in-popularity-two-years-after-its-launch/; TikTok Japan, https://note.com/tiktok/n/na6b2d4e3fcfa?hl=en)
- The merge chain is a known ladder (cherry, then strawberry, ... then watermelon), so the goal can be read at a glance. (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suika_Game)

### Vampire Survivors (2021–22)
- Level-up and chest audio-visual sequences "recur every ten to twenty seconds", and every run banks gold, so failure still moves progress forward. (Game Design Lab analysis, https://teemo.dev/game-design/vampire-survivors/systems/retention-and-engagement-systems/)
- It meets the PENS needs for competence and autonomy. Pacing alternates domination with tension, and "no run ever feels wasted". (The Conversation, https://theconversation.com/vampire-survivors-how-developers-used-gambling-psychology-to-create-a-bafta-winning-game-203613)
- The dev's background is slot machines, but the game is player-favourable: it caps a session at 30 minutes and has non-predatory monetisation. (Glover, https://benjaminglover.substack.com/p/how-a-game-rife-with-gambling-mechanics)
- It was playable free in the browser on itch.io. (Kotaku, https://kotaku.com/castlevania-roguelike-vampire-survivors-steam-itch-io-1848402308)

### Balatro (2024)
- Poker vocabulary was "an onboarding tool, a coat of paint": a familiar frame that lets players use deep mechanics right away. The real inspiration was Big Two. (Game Developer, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/localthunk-knew-balatro-needed-to-draw-players-in-with-poker)
- Escalating score targets, synergy combos (jokers) and huge numbers. Streamers (Northernlion) plus a demo drove it to 500k sales in 10 days. (Push Square, https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2024/03/smash-hit-indie-roguelike-balatro-has-sold-half-a-million-copies-in-10-days; PocketGamer.biz, https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-playstack-bet-on-balatro-and-won-big/)

### Among Us (2018, viral 2020)
- Considered finished, then revived by Twitch streamers (Sodapoppin, then xQc). AOC's stream peaked at 439k viewers. The **social clips** (accusations, betrayals) were the media. (Android Central, https://www.androidcentral.com/how-did-among-us-get-so-popular; GeekWire, https://www.geekwire.com/2020/aoc-helps-set-twitch-record-among-us-viral-game-developed-tiny-seattle-area-studio/)

### Block Blast (2024)
- About 210M installs in 2024 (second only to Roblox) and 40M daily players. (Pocket Gamer, https://www.pocketgamer.com/block-blast/40m-players/; Insightrackr, https://blog.insightrackr.com/en/docs/Nov24TopGames)
- Core: an 8×8 board and a tray of three pieces, with no timer. Placing pieces clears rows and columns. "Space has shape": the satisfying clear can be the wrong move, which gives depth from a shared visual object with no vocabulary. The visible tray creates a planning horizon without a tutorial. (Dashpop, https://dashpop.com/learn/deep-dives/block-blast)
- A **combo streak** multiplier rewards consecutive clears, with escalating audio and visuals, and the urge not to break the chain. (Funconomy, https://www.funconomy.com/post/block-blast-success-formula; Balancy, https://balancy.co/blog/2025/03/26/how-could-block-blast-by-hungry-studio-earn-more-monetization-and-gameplay-deconstruction/)
- Heavy TikTok and playable-ad acquisition, plus A/B testing. (Mobilegamer.biz, https://mobilegamer.biz/block-blast-maker-hungry-studio-a-b-tested-its-way-to-the-top-of-the-charts/)

### Royal Match and Candy Crush
- Wins through **speed and fairness**: one of the fastest switchers, with no loading between retries and short animations. Pieces are bright and saturated, distinct in **both colour and shape**, and bigger than rivals'. Generous power-ups, plus a shuffle instead of a forced bad move. (Deconstructor of Fun, https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2021/3/21/royal-match-the-new-king-from-turkey)
- Levels are designed to end in "almost wins", one or two moves short. One analysis says Royal Match has about twice the near-loss moments of Candy Crush. (DoF; Playliner, https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/difficulty-affects-engagement-revenue-match-3-games-playliner)

### Agar.io and Slither.io (2015–16)
- Agar.io was Google's third-biggest trending search of 2015, with 2B YouTube views. Valadares: "You don't need to read the instructions to start playing. The initial version didn't even have them." (WIRED UK, https://www.wired.com/story/agario-miniclip-game-viral-success-pewdiepie-youtube/)
- Free, in the browser, no install, an instant restart, and spread by YouTubers. (GamesBeat, https://gamesbeat.com/the-surprising-momentum-behind-io-games-like-agar-io/)

### Cookie Clicker (2013)
- Constant visible progress: the number always goes up, goals keep getting closer, and hidden upgrades and jokes reward curiosity. Offline progress gives a reason to return. (Deterding, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/146788/1/25_Deterding_draft_final_180515_SDE.pdf; Game Developer, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-recipe-behind-cookie-clicker)

### Grow a Garden (Roblox, 2025)
- Peaked at 22.3M concurrent players on 23 Aug 2025, and drove most of Roblox's Q2 growth. (How It Spread, https://howitspread.com/cases/grow-a-garden; Reuters, https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/viral-hit-grow-garden-pushes-roblox-past-100-million-users-lifts-bookings-2025-07-31/)
- Hooks: offline growth, rare seeds on a rotating stock, weekly updates and events. **Simple blocky crops with faces** were easy for fans to remake as characters and props in their own videos. (How It Spread; GameDiscoverCo, https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-grow-a-gardens-89-million-ccu)

## 2. Cross-cutting principles

1. **Seconds to fun.** Hyper-casual benchmarks: no login and no popups before play. Teach through play, not text, and make the early levels near-certain wins. (PocketGamer.biz, https://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-key-to-successful-hypercasual-prototypes/; Supersonic, https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/the-3-most-important-kpis-for-testing-your-hyper-casual-prototype/). Agar.io and Flappy Bird needed no instructions at all.
2. **Instant restart.** Flappy Bird, Agar.io and Royal Match (no loading between retries) all do this. A short death-to-retry time turns "ugh" into "one more".
3. **Near-misses.** These raise motivation to continue, especially when the player feels skill is involved. (Clark et al., https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.1774; replication, https://doi.org/10.1037/adb0000999). Royal Match tunes for almost-wins. Ethically we'll use honest near-misses, showing how close you got, never a rigged game.
4. **Variable rewards on a short cadence.** Something good every 10–20 s (Vampire Survivors). Combo escalation (Block Blast). Rare surprise outcomes (Grow a Garden rare seeds, Suika's big merges).
5. **Low floor, high ceiling.** Flappy Bird ("simple to learn, but you need skill"), Block Blast (fits are easy, planning is deep) and Balatro (poker frame, deep synergies).
6. **A familiar frame for onboarding.** Balatro's poker, Suika's fruit sizes, 2048's doubling numbers. A theme the player already understands explains the rules for free.
7. **Shareable, spoiler-free result.** Wordle's grid. It has to be plain text or emoji so it pastes into any group chat, show the shape of your run without spoilers, and carry a day number so everyone compares the same puzzle.
8. **Daily hook and streaks.** Wordle's single daily puzzle. Duolingo: users on a 7-day streak are 2.4× more likely to return the next day, and streak repair reduces drop-off after a break. (Duolingo, https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/; JCR, https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/49/6/1095/6572333)
9. **Clip-worthy moments.** Short-form video is a top discovery channel, and clips work when they show one hook in 15–60 s, vertical, readable with the sound off. (GameDiscoverCo, https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/video-games-and-tiktok-how-to-get and https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/everything-you-want-to-know-about). Suika's chain merges, Balatro's score explosions and Flappy's deaths are all built-in clips.
10. **Readable at a thumbnail glance.** Royal Match: saturated pieces distinct in colour **and** shape, big pieces, strong contrast with the board. Grow a Garden: simple shapes with faces.
11. **No-install web play.** 2048, Agar.io, Slither.io, Wordle and Vampire Survivors (itch.io). A link is the whole install.
12. **Juice.** "Juice It or Lose It" (Jonasson and Purho, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fy0aCDmgnxg): tweening, squash and stretch, particles, screen shake, sound, and characters that react. 2048 itself won on animation.
13. **Fairness and player-favourable design.** Royal Match's shuffle, Vampire Survivors' session cap, Wardle's no-dark-patterns. Players forgive difficulty, not cheating.
14. **Cute, characterful art.** Suika and Grow a Garden: faces on objects make them characters that fans remake.

## 3. Design requirements checklist (derived)

Every item must be met by the game design and traced in docs/design.md.

- [ ] **R1 One-sentence pitch** that a child can repeat. The rule fits in one line, with no jargon.
- [ ] **R2 Under 5 seconds from page load to first input.** No title-screen gate. The first board is live on load, and the first action is a near-certain win.
- [ ] **R3 Teach by doing.** At most one short line of instruction per step. No text walls. Ghost-hand hints that disappear once the player has acted.
- [ ] **R4 Under 1 second from game over to playing again**, with one tap. Never make the player wait for slow animations.
- [ ] **R5 Honest near-miss feedback.** On a loss, show exactly how close you were (for example "12 points short of your best", "1 cell from a clear"). Never rig outcomes.
- [ ] **R6 A reward beat every 5–20 s**: clears or merges with escalating combo feedback, plus rarer big moments (a mega combo or board wipe) every few minutes.
- [ ] **R7 Low floor, high ceiling.** The basic move is obvious, mastery comes from planning several moves ahead, and the score ceiling feels unbounded.
- [ ] **R8 A familiar frame** that explains the rules through its theme.
- [ ] **R9 A daily challenge.** Same seed for everyone, numbered (#N), one attempt per day counted, and a streak with a gentle repair (a freeze earned through play).
- [ ] **R10 A shareable, spoiler-free result**: text and emoji that paste anywhere, with the day number, a score shape and a URL that isn't spammy, plus an optional image card via the Web Share API or download.
- [ ] **R11 Clip-worthy moments.** Big chain reactions that look spectacular in a 9:16 crop and read with the sound off: large numbers, a screen flash, a slow-mo beat on the record moment.
- [ ] **R12 Thumbnail-readable art.** Pieces distinct in colour and shape, saturated against a calm background, big on mobile portrait, and recognisable at 64 px.
- [ ] **R13 Characterful pieces.** Faces or personality on the core objects, so they're memeable and remakeable.
- [ ] **R14 Juice everywhere**: tweened movement, squash and stretch, particles, light screen shake, layered sound with rising pitch on combos, haptics on mobile, and characters reacting.
- [ ] **R15 Layered progression, so no session is wasted**: per-run score, and meta XP and coins banked every run, unlocking cosmetics, themes, modes and achievements.
- [ ] **R16 Short sessions.** A run takes 3–8 minutes, and "one more" is always one tap away.
- [ ] **R17 Long tail**: endless high score, a long achievement list, collection completion, and a hard mode for experts.
- [ ] **R18 Fair and player-favourable.** No energy timers, no dark patterns, no ads. Offer a rescue once per run, earned through play.
- [ ] **R19 No-install web play.** Static site, fast load (under 300 KB of JS gzipped is the target), offline-capable via a service worker, and a meaningful link preview (Open Graph image).
- [ ] **R20 Return reasons**: the daily challenge, the streak, and a rotating weekly goal or event.
- [ ] **R21 Sound on by default but respectful**: a mute button always visible, and audio that starts on first input.
- [ ] **R22 Performance**: 60 fps on mid-range phones and no layout jank, with the canvas sized to the device pixel ratio but capped.
